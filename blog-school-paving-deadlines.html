Planning School Paving Around the School Calendar

School projects run on a calendar that doesn't move. Buses, students, and staff return on a set date, and every lot, walkway, and entrance needs to be ready for them.

Two jobs, one kind of deadline. We've completed parking lot paving for Hazleton Area School District ahead of the start of the school year, and sidewalk replacement and paving around Pocono Mountain West High School for Pocono Mountain School District, finished ahead of student orientation. At Pocono Mountain, the work brought our sitework, concrete, and paving crews together, coordinated on site by a project manager.

What to plan for. A few habits make a hard deadline more realistic. Set the finish date backward from the first day of school and leave a buffer for weather. Agree on what has to be done by that date (paving, striping, signage, accessible routes) and what can wait. Decide how students, buses, and deliveries will be kept apart from work areas while anything is still in progress. Schedule inspections and punch-list time before the deadline, not on it. And settle early who signs off and how issues will be handled.

Sequence matters. Site work, concrete, and paving depend on each other. Utilities and stone base typically come first, concrete work like curbs and sidewalks before final paving. When one trade slips, the next one waits. Coordinating the crews under a single contractor makes those handoffs easier to manage.

Start in the winter. If you're planning a summer project, talk to your contractor months ahead. Early scheduling, material ordering, and permitting make a September deadline far more realistic than starting the conversation in June.
